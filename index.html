introduction

Our proposal aims to explore biodegradable architectural shell structures through digital fabrication, skeletal construction, and differentiated material systems. Using parametric form-finding methods, the project combines natural fibers, bone glue, mycelium, bio-resins, and other compostable materials to create lightweight, self-supporting forms that gradually transform over time.

Material composition is strategically distributed according to structural and environmental requirements, producing a responsive material matrix rather than a uniform construction. By embracing weathering, deformation, and decay as part of the design process, the project questions permanence in architecture and investigates how digitally fabricated structures can exist as temporary, ecologically integrated systems.

MATERIALIZATION

Our proposal aims to explore materialization through the development of fully biodegradable, bio-based materials and differentiated material matrixes within an architectural shell structure. Using digital design and fabrication methods, the project investigates how varying densities, thicknesses, and composite mixtures can be strategically distributed according to structural, spatial, and environmental requirements.

Combining materials such as natural fibers, bio-resins, starch-based composites, and organic binders, the structure becomes a gradient system rather than a uniform construction. The proposal emphasizes localized material performance, reducing waste while allowing the pavilion to remain fully compostable at the end of its lifecycle. In doing so, the project examines how digital fabrication can support adaptive, ecologically responsive forms of material organization.

CONSTRUCTION

Our proposal aims to investigate skeletal construction methods through a lightweight shell-like structure made from wool and bone glue. Using digitally informed form-finding processes, the project explores how natural fibers and biodegradable binders can create self-supporting architectural forms with minimal material use. The wool is shaped and stiffened through layers of bone glue, producing a semi-rigid membrane that adapts into a curved, load-bearing shell structure.

Inspired by biological growth and anatomical frameworks, the pavilion emphasizes material efficiency, fragility, and tactility. Over time, the organic materials respond to humidity, tension, and environmental conditions, allowing the structure to subtly deform and evolve while questioning conventional distinctions between textile, sculpture, and architecture.

PROPOSAL

Our proposal aims to explore the relationship between digital fabrication, biodegradability, and temporal transformation through a sculptural architectural pavilion. Using parametric design methods and biodegradable materials such as mycelium, hemp fiber, bioplastics, and untreated wood, the structure is designed to gradually wither and decompose through exposure to weather and biological processes.

Rather than resisting decay, the project embraces deterioration as an active part of the work, allowing the pavilion to evolve over time. Initially appearing as a precise digitally fabricated form, it slowly transforms into an organic and unstable structure, questioning conventional ideas of permanence, material value, and sustainability in contemporary architecture and sculpture.
